## Inviolable rules — breaking any of these invalidates the thesis

1. **`data/raw/` is read-only.** No script may write, move, or modify it.
2. **Global seed = 42.** Every script calls `set_seed()` from `src/common/seed.py`
   as its first action.
3. **`data/splits/split_map_v1.json` is frozen once created.** Never regenerate,
   never re-shuffle, never "improve". It is committed to git on purpose.
4. **Gold-300 (G) is eval-only.** It never enters training, the RAG index,
   prompts, or threshold tuning.
5. **RAG index = R1 only.** R2 must never appear in retrieval.
6. **Verifier-B never enters the loop.** It is used for S6 scoring only. This
   wall *is* the Goodhart test — collapsing it makes RQ5 meaningless.
7. **No stemming, no stopword removal, no TF-IDF in the main pipeline.** LaBSE and
   BanglaBERT are contextual encoders and need natural text. (TF-IDF is allowed
   *only* as an explicitly-labelled cheap proxy in a pilot, never in a result.)
8. **Mirror, never merge.** English (IMDB/MPST) data never enters Bangla
   training, clustering, or RAG. No translate-train, no joint multilingual
   training. Merging changes the research question.
9. **UMAP is visualization-only.** Never cluster in UMAP space.
10. **No LLM is ever fine-tuned.** Generation is prompted only. Only 10 small
    artifacts get trained: 4 verifiers + 2 logistic regressions + 4 temperature
    scalings.

- **One config = one YAML = one result file.** If logic lives in a notebook cell,

  a verified number gets replaced by an unverified one.

## Literature: search Consensus first, and prefer recent work

**Standing instruction from Sabbir (2026-08-08).** Before proposing or defending
any method, **search the academic literature with Consensus** — not a plain web
search. Web search returns the famous paper; Consensus returns the field.

- **Recency is a requirement, not a preference.** This targets a Q1 journal in
  2026. A method defended only by a 2009 and a 2017 citation looks like it was
  chosen from memory, because it was. Always check what 2023–2026 says about a
  method *before* committing to it — the recent work frequently criticises the
  classic method, and finding that out after building is expensive.
- **Report what the search changed.** If the literature moved the design, say
  which paper and how. If it confirmed the design, say that too — a search that
  changed nothing is still evidence, and silently discarding it hides the check.
- Foundational citations are still needed for provenance. They are just not
  sufficient on their own.

### Search BEFORE the decision, not after — standing instruction (Sabbir, 2026-08-09)

The rule above says "before proposing or defending any method". **It is
strengthened here because the weak reading of it kept failing: search before
*every* design decision, including ones that feel like engineering rather than
science.** If a recommendation is about to be made and no search has happened,
the search happens first. A recommendation offered and then corrected costs more
than the search would have.

The distinction that kept getting lost: *"I already know this"* is not a reason
to skip the search. It is the condition under which the search is most likely to
change something, because unexamined confidence is exactly what stale training
data feels like from the inside.

**The record, kept because it is the argument for the rule:**

| Date | Decision made without searching first | What the search then said |
|---|---|---|
| ~2026-08-04 | G-300 rating scale | Kiritchenko & Mohammad (ACL 2017) had already shown rating scales less reliable than comparative judgements. The instrument failed its reliability gate, and attempt 2 used comparative judgement and worked. |
| 2026-08-07 | "mean ± SD over ≥3 seeds" as the S3.2 decision rule | Bethard (2022) classifies that exact use of seeds as **risky**, in a survey of 85 ACL Anthology papers. |
| 2026-08-09 | Recommending a single fixed learning rate for all 7 arms | Wen et al. (2025): blind hyperparameter transfer across methods is **unfair** — and Shehzad et al. (2023) show untuned baselines let *any* method be reported as winning. The recommendation was withdrawn. |

Three failures, three different topics, one shape: a plausible answer produced
from memory, then contradicted by a search that took two minutes.

### How to search — mechanics (Sabbir, 2026-08-09)

- **Consensus returns at most 10 results per call. Do not treat one call as "the
  search."** Run several narrower queries instead of one broad one, and vary the
  wording — the same question phrased two ways returns different papers, because
  the index matches terminology rather than intent. A design decision that rests
  on a single 10-result call rests on whatever that phrasing happened to surface.
- **Target 2025–2026 first.** Set `year_min` and read what the current field
  says before anything else. Older work may then be added for provenance, but it
  does not get to settle a question on its own.
- **If the recent window comes back thin, widen it — and say that you widened
  it.** A method whose only support is a 2017 paper may still be correct; the
  reader needs to know that is the situation, because "nothing recent exists" and
  "nothing recent was looked for" are different facts and look identical in a
  bibliography.
- Foundational citations still go in the bibliography. They just do not close a
  question that 2025–2026 work has since reopened.

---

This rule exists because it was broken once: the G-300 rating scale was designed
without checking, and Kiritchenko & Mohammad (ACL 2017) had already shown rating
scales to be less reliable than comparative judgements. **The failure was
predictable from the literature and the literature was not read.**

## How to behave

- **Do not invent numbers.** If a count, metric, or citation is not computed or
  present in a file, say so. Fabricated statistics are worse than no statistics.
- **Do not silently deviate from the pipeline.** If a step looks wrong or
  impossible, stop and say why. Deviations get logged in `docs/protocol.md`
  under "Deviations log" with a date and reason.
- **Negative results are results.** Never tune, filter, or reframe to make a
  number look better. The pre-committed three-outcome claims in `protocol.md`
  exist precisely so a bad number is still publishable.
- Prefer small, reviewable scripts over clever ones. This code is an appendix
  artifact that reviewers may read.
- Bangla text: never transliterate, never "normalize" characters beyond
  whitespace. Preserve the original script exactly.

## Known facts about the data (to be VERIFIED in Step 2, not assumed)

The pipeline's S0 table claims: 5,000 rows; labels 1665/1664/1670; 204 exact
duplicates; 72 reviews under 3 words; 1 null; usable ≈ 4,722; median 8 words;
zero emoji; zero URLs. **These are claims, not confirmed facts.** Step 2 exists
to confirm or refute them. If observed differs from claimed, the S0 table is
wrong and must be corrected — do not quietly adopt the claimed number.

There is **no movie-title column**, so reviews cannot be mapped to films. Any
design that assumes a held-out-films split is invalid.

## Open scientific risk to keep in mind

Reviews average ~8 words. The dominant signal LaBSE will capture is plausibly
**sentiment**, not engagement persona. The mandatory trap-check is
`ARI(cluster_labels, Sentiment)`, with thresholds and the claim to be made under
each outcome already pre-committed in `docs/protocol.md` (RQ1). Do not soften or
skip this check.

## Definition of Done

A step is not finished when the script runs. It is finished when the reasoning
behind its numbers is on record. Do **all six** at the end of every step:

**(a) Scaffold the lab-notebook entry.**

```bash
python src/common/step_close.py --step <S> --title "<what it did>" --feeds "<thesis section>" --results <result files>
```

**(b) Fill the TODOs by hand.** `step_close.py` scaffolds structure and pulls
scalars out of result JSONs — it never writes reasoning, and neither do you.
Auto-generated justification is plausible-sounding text nobody thought through,
which is exactly what a reviewer catches. **Where the decision was mine, ask me
— do not guess at my reasoning and write it down as though it were mine.**

**(c) Record new method references.** Anything used here that needs a citation
goes into `docs/related_work.md` *and* `docs/references.bib`.

**(d) Update `docs/STATUS.md`** — step status, any new verified fact, any open
decision opened or closed. Update it in the same commit as the notebook entry.

**(e) Add a Deviations row to `docs/protocol.md`** if the step departed from the
spec in any way, with date and reason.

**(f) `python src/common/step_close.py --check` must exit 0.** The `.githooks/pre-commit`
hook runs this automatically.

**Commit the notebook entry, the results, and the code together.** A commit that
touches `results/` without touching `docs/lab_notebook.md` is a defect, not a
style preference: a number whose reasoning was never written down cannot be
defended in the thesis, and reconstructing it weeks later is guesswork. The
`--check` step enforces this on the staged diff.
