# Appendix B — Human evaluation and ethics record

## B.1 Construct validation

The first instrument used a 0–3 ordinal rating and failed its reliability gate
(ordinal alpha 0.4970). Its downstream validity gate was not run. The second,
comparative instrument used 50 four-way length-matched intrusion sets and a
separate 40-item direction task. The two annotators achieved 0.780/0.840 on
intrusion and 0.850/0.850 on direction. Both the failed and successful
instruments remain reported.

## B.2 Generated-output evaluation

- Participants: three adults, native Bangla speakers, university batchmates of
  the researcher; public identifiers A/B/C only.
- Material: one frozen balanced 100-item subset, five items from every
  condition × target-level cell.
- Task: forced Level-0/Level-1 classification under blinded condition labels.
- Registered judgments: 300/300.
- Pooled target match: 0.9133, 95% item-bootstrap CI [0.8667, 0.9567].
- Raw three-way agreement: 0.8800.
- Nominal Krippendorff alpha: 0.8405, 95% item-bootstrap CI
  [0.7473, 0.9200].

This validates requested-level recoverability on the subset. It is not a rating
of overall quality, naturalness, plot faithfulness or audience preference and
does not support condition ranking at five items per cell.

## B.3 Consent and data handling

Participants were 18+, received the task and voluntary-participation statement,
and provided consent. No monetary honorarium was paid; refreshments were
provided. Names and consent evidence remain private. Repository responses use
codes only and contain no demographic variables beyond eligibility facts.

The convenience relationship creates possible perceived social pressure. The
thesis therefore states that participation/withdrawal had no academic or
personal consequence and does not describe the sample as representative of all
Bangla speakers.

## B.4 Reporting boundary

No claim of institutional approval, exemption, or formal review is made. The
defensible record is limited to adult eligibility, voluntary informed consent,
private consent evidence, coded responses, and the convenience-relationship
limitation above. This section documents what occurred; it does not infer an
institutional classification absent from the evidence.
