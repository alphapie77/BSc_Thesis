Even with the progress made, the majority of earlier works still concentrated on analyzing the time after release or used aggregated sentiment polarity, which caused them to miss
the specific persona-related differences that form the various audience segments. The restrictions imposed by such limitations render the existing methods inapplicable for pre-release
decision-making, where understanding the different audience expectations is of utmost importance. This situation is more serious in the case of low-resource languages like Bengali,
where the lack of labeled persona-specific datasets, combined with rich linguistic variability create further hurdles for automated sentiment modeling. The recent advancements in
retrieval-augmented generation and neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence are making it clear
that the merging of structured knowledge representations with neural models would lead
to improving contextual grounding, interpretability, and reasoning capabilities in language
tasks. The present work, inspired by these insights, suggests a neuro-symbolic multi-agent
framework that not just analyzes sentiment patterns but also mimics audience reactions with
respect to persona-awareness prior to a movie’s release, thus filling a major gap in the prediction of audience for low-resource language contexts at the pre-release stage.
1.2 Motivation
The main reason for doing this research is a significant shortcoming in the Bengali film
industry: the lack of automated, data-driven tools to forecast pre-release reactions of the
audience. At present, filmmakers completely depend on traditional focus groups and their
instincts, which not only are slow but also come at a hefty cost and, often, cannot even recognize the different viewpoints of the various audience groups. The conventional systems for
analyzing sentiments are not good enough for such languages as Bengali, which have limited
labeled datasets and where linguistically speaking it is very difficult to come up with personaspecific predictions due to the nuances involved. This work proposes the development of a
neuro-symbolic multi-agent framework that allows for the explainable, persona-aware text
generation by integrating the contextual understanding of neural language models with the
structured reasoning of symbolic systems. The model is built such that weak supervision can
be utilized in a way where the audience can be identified in different segments without needing a large amount of manual labeling, whereas at the same time the retrieval-augmented
generation (RAG) method ensures the reviews created are both contextually grounded and
