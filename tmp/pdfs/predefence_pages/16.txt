Chapter 2
Related Work
This chapter surveys current research studies relevant to the present work. It presents main
contributions to existing research while identifying research gaps which the proposed work
will address. The literature is categorized into four key areas: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks, Agentic Systems and Personas, Neuro-Symbolic AI, and Bangla
Natural Language Processing.
2.1 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Frameworks
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become a fundamental technique for enhancing Large Language Models. Lewis et al. [5] originally presented RAG models utilizing
a pre-trained seq2seq model as a parametric memory system alongside a dense vector index. Their work achieved state-of-the-art results on open-domain QA tasks. Brown et al.
[6] further noted that RAG functions to decrease hallucinations and outdated knowledge by
using retrieved proof to validate LLM results, although empirical data shows inconsistent
outcomes due to varying assessment methods.
To address these inconsistencies and improve performance, several advanced frameworks have been proposed. Zhao et al. [7] provided a complete examination of RAG methods
applied to AIGC use cases, establishing foundation categories for retriever-enabled generator enhancement. Specific improvements include the work of Chang et al. [8], who proposed
MAIN-RAG. This approach uses inter-agent consensus for dependable document selection,
achieving a 2-11 percent enhancement in answer accuracy. Similarly, Yao et al. [9] introduced a framework using reflective tags to control the retrieval process, effectively reducing
hallucinations by focusing on high-scoring documents.
