Count Vectorization:
Count Vectorization is an approach utilized in the fields of natural language processing and
text analysis for the transformation of text data into numerical vectors, which could be comprehended by machine learning algorithms. The technique consists of calculating the number of occurrences of each word in a document or a whole corpus and thereby depicting
these occurrences as vectors. The operation of Count Vectorization may be mathematically

The preprocessing pipeline successfully removed noisy, duplicate, and low-quality entries while preserving linguistically rich Bangla reviews. The final dataset exhibited wellbalanced word-length distributions and strong textual consistency which made it suitable for
three different research purposes.
3.3.2 Semantic Embedding Generation
The research utilized the Language-agnostic BERT Sentence Embedding (LaBSE) model,
which exists in the sentence-transformers library, to create sentence-level embeddings
that capture the semantic meaning of Bengali text. The researchers converted each review
into a 768-dimensional dense vector, which resulted in an embedding matrix of 6,114 rows
and 768 columns. These embeddings make it possible to calculate similarity and perform
clustering in environments that use multiple languages and have limited resources, including
the Bangla language.
3.3.3 Persona Discovery via K-Means Clustering
Unsupervised clustering was performed on the generated embeddings using the K-Means
algorithm with 𝐾 = 3. The choice of three clusters was motivated by interpretability and
alignment with audience engagement levels observed in Bengali cinema discourse. The
clustering process grouped reviews into three distinct semantic clusters representing different audience behavior patterns. The resulting clusters consisted of 2,773 (45.4%), 1,964
(32.1%), and 1,377 (22.5%) reviews, respectively, indicating a natural stratification of audience engagement within the dataset.
